## 1. Executive decision
V2 proved the core concept: 8 official central-bank benchmarks, 28 forex pair gaps, dated outlook observations, pair-specific central-bank policy updates, source-linked next decisions, forward-rate calculation and 1D/7D repricing capability (without licensed forward feeds yet).
V3 must turn those functions into a fast, credible, navigable site for macro-aware FX swing traders. The **homepage is an entry and discovery screen, not the full research application**. The **pair page is the daily-use research destination**. The **28-pair comparison is a separate full-screen directory/table**. This promotes directly addressable, shareable, internally linked, search-indexable research.

Top 5 product objectives:
1. Select a pair and see its dated, verified policy position within **two interactions** from home.
2. Reach the next two relevant policy decisions and original sources without searching eight-bank material.
3. Clearly distinguish actual policy rates, derivatives-implied expectations, forecasts, and analyst/source commentary.
4. Make every public page useful *without client-side JavaScript* for essential facts, SEO and accessibility.
5. Increase qualified repeat usage and organic discoverability without thin autogenerated pages or invented trading advice.

## 4. Audience and trader jobs-to-be-done
### Primary
A global forex swing/position trader tracking EUR/USD, GBP/USD, USD/JPY, AUD/USD and crosses. They want to answer quickly:
1. What is the CURRENT official rate gap?
2. Which two policy benchmarks and announcement dates underpin it?
3. What is priced for the NEXT 3/6/12 months, IF correctly licensed and available?
4. What changed in reported expected pricing after CPI/jobs/central-bank news?
5. Which related official meeting is next, and when?
6. Can I bookmark/share this pair and check tomorrow?
### Secondary
Macro students and research-oriented visitors, needing explanation and original sources. Not an execution venue, charting broker, general financial portal or trade-signals product.
### Primary journey
Search/entry → clear summary → choose pair → pair hub with current rate gap / two banks / next decisions / latest policy updates / forward-data status → bookmark or inspect source/methodology. No login required.

## 6. Recommended homepage
### Hero: calm, useful, not promotional
**H1:** "Compare central-bank rates across 28 forex pairs."
**Subheading:** "See the verified policy-rate gap, relevant bank decisions and what markets have reported about the next move. Source-linked. No trading signals."
- Main interaction: prominently labelled "Find a currency pair" search/autocomplete, accepting EURUSD and EUR/USD.
- Fast picks: EUR/USD, GBP/USD, USD/JPY, AUD/USD, USD/CAD, AUD/JPY. Users can also browse all 28.
- Primary action "Explore a pair" (prefer actually navigating to a selected pair); secondary text link "Compare all 28 pairs".
- Small, trustworthy "Policy data checked [date UTC] · Official bank sources"; if market-implied feed unavailable: "Forward-rate feed: Not connected", not "Live."
- Avoid visual hero video, generic stock forex imagery, AI-prediction assertions, high-noise tickers and large marketing banners.
### Below hero — one-screen hierarchy
1. **Policy snapshot**: three compact cards: biggest rate gap; latest bank policy decision (dated); next verified decision (with precise timezone where available).
2. **Featured rate gaps / Explore pairs**: A compact, interactive homepage comparison defaulting to the **seven USD majors**, with alternate tabs for **Widest policy-rate gaps** and **Closest policy rates**, then one obvious **Compare all 28 pairs →** link. The exact ranking, data labels and hierarchy are specified below. Never misrepresent these as "best trades", expected FX gains or higher carry yields.
3. **What changed**: 2–3 verified/dated market observations, explicit data types and no causal overclaiming; no auto-assigned directional bias.
4. **Upcoming policy decisions**: next 3–4 official scheduled bank meetings, link to /decisions/.
5. **Learn / methodology**: three carefully written guides and a short "What does 150 bp mean?" primer.
### Homepage pair spotlight — founder review addition, 2026-10-16
**Recommended hierarchy:** (1) Hero + quick pair search, (2) familiar major-pair overview, (3) optional widest/closest policy-gap ranking, (4) most recent documented changes and the next decisions, (5) concise learning links. Do not start the homepage with only the highest-gap cross pair; many first-time visitors begin with EUR/USD, GBP/USD or USD/JPY.

The homepage preview has a prominent accessible three-option segmented control:
- **Major pairs (default)** — seven familiar USD pairs in a predictable, named ordering: EUR/USD, GBP/USD, USD/JPY, AUD/USD, NZD/USD, USD/CAD, USD/CHF. Major here means USD pairs, not the seven biggest gaps. Show 7 rows on desktop, 4–5 with a Show More interaction on small phones, with an always-visible All 28 destination.
- **Widest gaps** — five pair combinations with the **largest absolute difference** in currently VERIFIED official policy-rate benchmarks; order by absolute bps descending. This is an objective mathematical ranking, not highest potential trade, current carry or forecast strength.
- **Closest rates** — five pair combinations with the **smallest absolute policy-rate gap**; order by absolute bps ascending. The label "Closest policy rates" is more accurate than "Lowest divergence trades" and avoids implying imminent convergence or that a small gap is favorable.

Table columns: **Pair (real link to canonical URL)**, **Signed current gap (bp)**, and **Higher policy-rate currency**; desktop may show base/quote benchmark rates, with effective/as-of date on hover or beneath section. On mobile use a compact vertical list with pair + signed bp, an optional one-line benchmark context and readable touch targets, not six crowded columns. Rank using unchanged `getPairs` calculations, preserving market-standard base/quote orientation, stable tie-breaking, correct +/- signs and explicit "policy-rate gap" unit labels.
Visible section subhead: **"Official policy-rate differences — not signals or market forecasts."** Display data checked date/UTC and official-source methodology link; do not label a static policy snapshot "live". If data is stale, display a clear stale status and source link rather than presenting current ranks as fresh. If two pairs tie, retain deterministic tie-break and do not invent a reason for ranking.
Use server-rendered semantic `<a href="/pairs/eur-usd/">` links for all featured pairs and a crawlable link to **/rate-gaps/**; switching the segmented control can progressively enhance but must not be the only way crawlers or keyboard visitors access the other 28 pairs. Render an indexable static list (or equivalent HTML) and enhance client-side as needed.
**Future enhancement, not launch default:** "Biggest 24h/7d change in expected gap" only after licensed comparable time-series snapshots are actually populated. Do not use a market-pricing panel that appears full but is driven by outdated dated Reuters snippets.
**Measure:** pair-link click-through by ranking tab, pair search-to-visit success, visits to full comparison, repeat interest in widest vs closest. If the data shows traders rarely use Closest rates, demote it from the homepage without removing full comparison sorting.

Do not put the complete pair decision timelines, eight full bank cards, every policy record and 28-row detailed data grid in the home opening flow.
The existing homepage's long four-tab application should be reorganized behind dedicated routes; preserve the underlying tested calculations.

## 7. 28 pair-page template (V3 core)
Canonical: /pairs/eur-usd/; title example "EUR/USD Interest Rate Gap, ECB vs Fed, Next Decisions | TRADE90".
Content:
- Breadcrumb Home > Currency Pairs > EUR/USD, page H1, pair switcher and fully readable date/source status.
- First fold: ECB official benchmark, Fed official benchmark, **signed base-minus-quote bp**, absolute gap, clear "Higher policy benchmark: [currency]" factual label. No direction inference.
- Small baseline explainer "Rate gap is not spot FX, future return or broker swap".
- "Market-implied 3M / 6M / 12M": when verified/licensed input exists, rates, timestamps, source instruments and method; otherwise concise "Not available" with explanatory links; don't consume an entire page with 28 dashes.
- "Next major decisions to watch": only EUR + USD (two cards), official calendar links; no old meeting mislabelled upcoming.
- "Central Bank Policy Updates": only ECB + Fed official publications; date, source, summary; proper maximum/expand-more UX.
- "What changed for this pair": source-comparable expected gap revisions, if available; published dated observations labelled as reported when otherwise.
- "Why this pair's rate gap matters": 3–5 paragraphs of unique factual context, no templated generic fluff, no signal.
- "History & methodology": date sequence only when real validated historic observations exist; citations, caveats and optional FAQs.
- Related pairs with same currencies and contextual links back to full pair directory.
Every important value must be present in **server-rendered HTML**, and dynamic switches must not conceal the only searchable version of material from crawlers.

## 8. Eight central-bank hub pages

Never construct fake historic levels or imply policy projections are market-implied forecasts.

## 9. Design system, accessibility, reading mode
Retain original TRADE90 branding. Offer **System / Light / Dark** themes and a visible mobile-accessible toggle. Preserve user choice, avoid theme flash and ensure all forms, tables, notices and evidence badges have WCAG AA contrast. Default to system preference; dark for charts/dense terminals, light for reading longer guides if the user chooses.
- Light mode: off-white background (#f8fafc), near-black text (#0f172a), thin slate borders, emerald as restrained accent, never emerald text on insufficient contrast.
- Dark mode: current #0f172a base, slightly elevated panels, clear light text; avoid dozens of equally saturated green items.
- Mobile first: 44px targets; no tiny 0.62rem nav; show pair, signed gap and a details link, collapse less important columns; horizontal scrolling only within explicitly marked data tables.
- Keyboard and screen reader paths, focus indication, meaningful status labels, no color-only signal semantics, reduced-motion compliance.
- Make "as-of" visually prominent, with distinct "Published", "Observed", "Updated" timestamps.
- Add watchlist/recently viewed pairs without sign-in as a later progressive enhancement (local on-device preference, with privacy notice if applicable).

## 10. SEO, entity trust, technical discoverability
### Highest priority
- Verify Google Search Console property, index status, Googlebot render, live robots.txt, sitemap XML content-types, canonical pages, domain redirects (www/non-www, slash variants), 404/410 response codes and internal linking. The site's Astro config declares sitemap/robots generators; this review **did not verify production delivery** of those files.
- Inventory old indexed /research/, /calculator/, /tools/, /blog/ and legacy content URLs using GSC, server logs and an export. **301 only when the target meets substantially the same user intent and supplies a useful replacement**, 410/404 otherwise. No blanket redirects to home.
- Remove/refresh outdated site claims from accessible content, Open Graph images, titles, search snippets and old PWA caches; allow recrawling and request indexing for key replacements. Do not invent founding credentials or republish misleading legacy bios.
- New pair and bank pages need stable crawlable href links, distinct titles, headings, meta descriptions, self canonicals, valid sitemaps, **unique factual content** and structured data that exactly matches visible content.
- Appropriate schema can include Organization, WebSite, BreadcrumbList and Article for authored guides; Dataset only if genuinely applicable with real provenance/licensing, not as a shortcut to rankings. No fabricated AggregateRating, FAQ rich-result claims or pseudo-live timestamps.
### Content topics / clusters
1. 28 original pair research pages anchored to useful data, not 28 near-duplicate boilerplates.
2. 8 bank rate/meeting pages supported by primary sources.
3. Explain the meaning of signed versus absolute spread, Fed target midpoint, ECB deposit rate, carry versus policy gap, market-implied expectations, CPI repricing, FOMC probabilities, swap versus OIS, different decision calendars.
4. Useful date-specific primary-sourced research updates with genuine changes, not recycled "latest" articles.
5. English first for global trader intent; multilingual only if professionally translated and maintained with hreflang + canonical correctness.
Content content-quality gate: correct formula + bank source + timestamp + actual unique value + clear caveat + editorial ownership. Avoid scaled programmatic SEO pages with no unique utility.

## 11. Search visibility in AI interfaces (AEO / GEO)
Interpret "AGI and AGM" intent as **Answer Engine Optimization (AEO)** and **Generative Engine Optimization (GEO)** alongside normal SEO, not speculative special AI ranking tags.
Google's Search Central guidance says AI Overviews/Mode rely on basic SEO, accessible text and indexing; there is no required special AI schema or magic ai.txt file. Bing exposes an AI Performance report useful for monitoring citations.
- Put concise, precise Q&A explanations next to pair data, with clear units, dates, source links and decision identity.
- Add source-attributed factual definitions, original math examples, factual comparison tables and self-contained key conclusions.
- Provide unique stable URLs so AI search can link to a particular bank/pair claim.
- Mark data availability and freshness visibly; correct the source when necessary; never spoof authors or expertise.
- Record AI citation visibility in Bing Webmaster Tools and relevant referral events. Eligibility does not guarantee impressions, citations or traffic.

## 12. Data and trust gates
Publish three different data categories with unmistakable labels:
**Official** — verified current bank benchmarks/decisions.
**Market-implied** — licensed futures/OIS-derived normalized forward policy levels, same horizon, synchronized timestamps, redisplay authorization.
**Reported/forecast** — Reuters-attributed meeting probability report, economist consensus or official policy projection, labelled separately and dated.
- No implied future rate calculations if either currency lacks a comparable verified licensed input.
- The upcoming bank decisions calendar is currently curated; must flag last review and validate event dates by source. If a date is past, don't present as upcoming. Revalidate holiday/emergency meeting changes.
- Establish automated ingestion, immutable observations, source failure monitoring, alerting and human corrections policy **before** calling the service "live".
- Make status "Verified snapshot" versus "Live market feed" explicit. No asserted real-time updates before infrastructure is actually connected.

## 13. Growth model — stretch target 10,000 visits/day
10,000 visits/day ~= 300,000/month. This is a **stretch objective**, not a forecast based on measured current traffic (GA4/GSC baseline unavailable in review). Do not assume the 28 pages will deliver 10,000 daily visits by themselves.
Growth flywheel: targeted evergreen search guides → useful bank/pair hubs → returning watchlist/event users → direct visits and citations/referrals; distribute standout source-linked comparisons on FX communities, social and relevant finance newsletters without spam or clickbait.
Plan phases:
- **P0 first 2 weeks:** analytics baseline + index cleanup + editorial fact check + theme/UX user testing decisions.
- **P1 weeks 3–6:** new IA, hero, pair selector, full index and first 7 unique major pair pages; full desktop/mobile validation.
- **P2 weeks 7–12:** all 28 substantial pages, 8 bank hubs, central-bank calendar, internal-link and schema QA; useful weekly source-linked policy research.
- **P3 months 4–6:** benchmark organic impressions/clicks and meaningful returning users; release source-licensed forward pricing when acquired; improve on query/retention evidence.
- **P4 months 6–18:** systematic editorial expansion and distribution. Assess 10,000/day only against actual acquisition and retention curves.
Progressive checkpoints are **illustrative planning gates** not promises: measurable first 100/day → 500/day → 1,000/day → 3,000/day → 10,000/day; optimize a smaller number of genuinely successful useful pages rather than multiplying thin pages.
Demand + click reality: revenue and traffic are secondary to trust; instrument conversion to useful engagement and revisits.

## 14. Analytics scorecard and experiments
Track GA4 or privacy-conscious equivalent plus Search Console + Bing Webmaster Tools:
- Search: valid indexed pair pages, nonbrand impressions/clicks, queries & ranking distribution, organic sessions from /pairs/ and /learn/.
- Engagement: hero pair-search usage, pair-page views, pair-switch rate, clicked sources, decision-calendar engagement, bounce/engaged sessions, returning 7-/30-day users, bookmarks/watchlist.
- Quality: stale source observations, missing forecast coverage, correction response, source link failures, policy decision update lag.
- UX: task success for "find EUR/USD Fed/ECB rates" in under ~30 sec, no mobile horizontal page overflow, visible data status, responsive controls.
- Performance: LCP <=2.5s, INP <=200ms, CLS <=0.1 at p75 **as recommended targets**, not measured scores. Audit actual CWV and real-user field data.
- Growth: total daily unique users, sessions/day, organic share, direct share, retained visitors, newsletter opt-ins if genuinely offered; segment by geography and device.
Use controlled A/B or staged before/after comparisons for home hero and table density only after sufficient volumes. No meaningless vanity claims.
